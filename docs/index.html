Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring Using Search Console Signals

A machine-learning approach to prioritising content refresh actions from GSC data
Marwan Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring September 2026 79M rows · 104 clients
Abstract Introduction Data Methodology Results Limitations Recommendations Reproducibility Acknowledgments

Abstract

Content teams managing large websites face a prioritisation problem: which pages should be refreshed first? This paper investigates whether Google Search Console signals — impressions, clicks, average position, CTR, and data coverage — can identify pages likely to gain clicks after a content refresh. Using 79 million rows of pseudonymised production search data from the FlyRank internship warehouse, a Random Forest classifier was trained on March 2026 data and evaluated on April–May 2026, achieving ROC-AUC of 0.82 under a client-grouped validation split — a measured improvement over a hand-written rule baseline (AUC 0.64). The model is presented as a decision-support tool: it surfaces a ranked queue of refresh candidates for human review, not an automated action trigger. Impressions volume and data-coverage consistency were observed as the strongest directional signals; CTR and position, despite being the basis of the hand-written rule, contributed less under permutation importance on held-out data.

Introduction

FlyRank's content clients face a recurring triage problem: large websites accumulate pages that were once well-ranked but gradually lose organic traffic, and editorial teams have no scalable way to decide which pages to refresh first.

This work asks: can a model trained on Search Console signals rank refresh candidates better than a hand-written rule? The target is not prediction in the causal sense — we cannot observe whether a refresh caused growth. Instead, we define a proxy label (click growth ≥ 20% month-over-month) and measure how well the model ranks true-positive pages above false positives relative to a rule baseline.

The approach follows FlyRank's internship research track: frame honestly, validate carefully, and deliver a human-reviewed playbook rather than an automated pipeline.

Methodology

Label definition

Binary label: did a page's total monthly clicks grow by ≥ 20% from the feature month to the label month? This is a proxy — growth may be caused by content quality, seasonal demand, or algorithm updates. The label captures "growth happened," not "a refresh caused it."

Features

FeatureConstructionKnowable at decision time?
gsc_impressionsSUM over feature monthYes — past month total
gsc_clicksSUM over feature monthYes — past month total
gsc_avg_positionAVG over feature monthYes — GSC ~3-day lag
ctrclicks / impressionsYes — derived from past data
days_with_dataCOUNT DISTINCT report_dateYes — past count

Baseline (W04 hand-written rule)

Score = (expected_ctr − actual_ctr) × gsc_impressions, where expected CTR is a fixed lookup by position bucket (top-3: 0.48%, top-10: 0.35%, top-20: 0.28%, beyond-20: 0.13%). Reason codes: LOW_CTR_GOOD_POSITION → REFRESH_TITLE_META; HIGH_IMP_LOW_CTR → REFRESH_CONTENT.

Model

Random Forest Classifier — 100 estimators, max depth 6, random state 42. Chosen because the feature relationships are non-linear and the model produces calibrated probabilities for ranking.

Validation design

Two splits were compared to show the before/after effect of validation rigour:

  • Time-aware split (W05): train on 2026-03 features, test on 2026-04 features. AUC 0.77.
  • Client-grouped split (W06): 80% of clients in train, remaining 20% in test — no client seen in both. AUC 0.82. This is the honest number.

Leakage audit

All five features were verified as observable before the label window opens. Feature-label correlations were checked — highest observed was 0.10 (days_with_data), consistent with a real but modest signal, not leakage.

Limitations

  • Label does not capture causation. Click growth may result from seasonal demand, algorithm updates, or competitor changes — not a content refresh. The model identifies growth-likely pages, not refresh-caused-growth pages.
  • GSC-only features. GA4 sessions and engagement signals were excluded due to high NaN rates across clients. Adding GA4 signals for clients who have them connected may improve ranking quality.
  • Single-month feature window. Features are aggregated over one month. Trend features (month-over-month change) were not included and may capture decay signals the current model misses.
  • High false-negative rate. The model misses a large share of pages that do grow. It is better suited to precision-first triage (finding reliable candidates) than recall-first discovery (finding every opportunity).
  • No refresh event log. The warehouse has no column recording when a page was actually updated. The label conflates organic growth with refresh-driven growth.

Human review rules

  • Inspect every page in the top 50 before any action is taken.
  • Verify search intent manually before rewriting any REFRESH_CONTENT page.
  • Do not act on the score alone — read the page.

No-go list — do not automate

  • Pages with fewer than 3 clicks in the feature month
  • Pages published less than 60 days ago
  • Pages with a pending client redesign or migration
  • Any title or content change without explicit client approval
  • Automated publishing of any kind

Monitoring triggers

  • Retrain if AUC on the latest month drops below 0.65
  • Retrain if positive label rate shifts more than 5 percentage points from 16.2%
  • Retrain if GA4 data becomes available for more clients
  • Re-run scoring monthly on the latest closed month
